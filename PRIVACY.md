# Privacy draft — development alpha

Butler is private by default. This document describes the code in this repository; it is not a finalized public contribution policy.

- By default, the microphone opens only while the push-to-talk shortcut is held after its 160 ms tap threshold. Optional “Hey Butler” mode keeps the microphone active for local wake-phrase recognition; enable it in Settings or the menu bar and disable it from the menu bar at any time. Background speech is discarded inside the native helper and never enters the app, diagnostic stream, task history, or model requests. Apple Speech requires on-device recognition, with no cloud fallback. Audio buffers and the stream of partial transcripts are not saved or uploaded. Completed commands/corrections after activation become encrypted local history and reach the configured task model. If recognition ends with an empty final result after release or the silence endpoint, the latest nonempty hypothesis is used as the completed command; it is marked as recovered and cannot authorize an approval. Hands-free mode automatically rearms after a command, cancellation, or recoverable speech error, until disabled or the app quits. It suspends on system sleep/session deactivation and secure input. With follow-up listening on, the microphone also transcribes for a few seconds without the wake phrase right after a turn, a question or an approval prompt; nothing is emitted to the app until speech is detected in that window.
- Spoken replies use the on-device Mac voice by default. The optional free natural voice also runs entirely on this Mac; downloading it contacts huggingface.co and raw.githubusercontent.com once for the model files, and no text or audio is sent. The optional natural voice sends only the reply text (never audio, typed text, credentials, full web addresses or file paths) to OpenAI with your key, only in Bring-your-own-model mode, and is labelled as an AI-generated voice. Replies are audible to people nearby; turn them off in Settings.

- Text updates over iMessage are off until you turn them on and save your own number, which is kept in the encrypted configuration and never read from `.env` at runtime. When they are on, Butler texts that one handle short lines about run moments only: started, needs approval (the policy question, never typed text or coordinates), paused or handed back with the reason, finished with the model's summary, failed. Never screenshots, partial transcripts, step-by-step narration, credentials, full web addresses or file paths; at most eight lines per task and twenty per hour. By default only tasks you start by text send updates at all. Replies are read by opening the local Messages database `~/Library/Messages/chat.db` read-only, which needs Full Disk Access; only messages from your configured handle, in a one-to-one chat, newer than the moment you switched the feature on and less than five minutes old are read, and a message body is copied out of the database only after those checks pass. Nothing is read when “Let me start and stop tasks by text” is off. Message text is never written to diagnostics or run history; a texted task becomes the task text of a normal run. Sending uses the Messages app and needs macOS Automation permission. iMessage content is carried by Apple between your devices under Apple's terms, and anyone holding your phone can read it: that is the trade you are making by turning this on.
- The phone remote is off until you turn it on, and works only over your own Tailscale network: the page is served from this Mac on its Tailscale address, never on the internet, and Butler never enables Tailscale Funnel or Serve. Every connection is checked against Tailscale's record of which device and account it comes from (only your own login; never this Mac itself, a tagged or shared node), and a phone can do nothing until you allow it in Settings on this Mac. What the phone can see: what the Mac is doing, the current approval question and the shape of the step (a control's label, a menu path, "typing 12 characters"), short progress lines, and, only if you choose, a blurred 24-pixel mosaic of the selected display (protected apps and secure fields excluded). What it can send: stop, pause, continue, plain-language instructions, and, only if you allow that phone to, approvals for routine steps (opening, quitting, navigation); sending, saving, deleting, paying, installing, signing in, settings and system changes are approved only on the Mac, and never while someone is using it. Instructions from the phone go to your configured model the way a typed task does and become task or correction text in your encrypted run history; credentials in a line are refused. Butler keeps the list of phones you allowed (device name and Tailscale node identifier) in the encrypted configuration, stores the page's TLS certificate encrypted on this Mac, and logs no message text, names, addresses or tokens. Tailscale sees which of your devices connect to which, not what they send. Anyone holding your unlocked phone with an allowed device can do what you allowed it; "Lock phone remote" in Settings or the menu bar cuts every phone off at once.

- Private local uses an Ollama endpoint at a literal loopback address. Prompts, screenshots and actions do not pass through CoArena. Use a locally downloaded model. The client cannot establish what a separately installed or modified inference service does internally.
- Private BYOM sends the task, completed corrections, current screenshot and compact action/result history directly to the configured provider. That provider's policies apply. OpenAI Responses requests set `store: false`; this is not a general claim of zero provider retention.
- The "Decide with Jev" setting (Bring-your-own-model only) sends what the dialog model is sent for a free-form turn to OpenRouter and TypeSafe, with zero data retention requested and no fallback provider: your words, the recent conversation (including earlier replies that read out notifications or on-screen results), the current or last task and its result, and the agenda and open app names when those are on; never a screenshot. It runs only after you agree to that: by typing an OpenRouter key into its Settings field (beside this disclosure), ticking its toggle, or launching a developer build with `--decide-with-jev`. A key imported from a `.env` is never agreement on its own. Unticking it, or "Remove key", turns it off and keeps it off. Its only effect is to start your own words sooner; see docs/EVALS.md.
- Model observations also include bounded app/window titles, selected text and visible accessibility labels, up to 12 open/recently observed window references, eight previously observed document basenames, and three recent local tasks. Secure controls/protected applications are skipped and detected credential text is removed, but this is not complete content anonymization. These fields are stored with encrypted local frames and excluded from contributions. There is no filesystem crawl, browser-history read, or continuous background screenshot recorder; recent document references accumulate from active observations.
- Each observation also lists the applications you have open with up to three window titles each (at most 14 applications), and the frontmost application's menus with their shortcuts (titles only, never menu contents such as recent file names beyond the menu's own labels). Protected applications are never listed.
- With “Read my notifications” on (off by default; Settings › Learning), the native helper watches Notification Center and reads each banner's application name, title and text when it appears on screen. Nothing is read after the fact and no notification database is opened. The last twelve from the past four hours are kept in the helper's memory only, never written to disk or diagnostics, and go to your model as bounded, credential-redacted context lines. Notifications whose application matches a protected app are not read. Turning the setting off stops reading and clears the list immediately.
- “Use my Calendar and Reminders” is off until you turn it on, which asks macOS for Calendar and Reminders access for a separate helper (`coarena-agenda`), so this grant is independent of Screen Recording and Accessibility. When on, each task reads the titles, times and calendar names of events from now to the end of tomorrow (at most eight) and the titles, due dates, list names and priority of open reminders that are overdue, undated or due within a week (at most ten). Notes, locations, attendees and links are never read, and nothing is ever created, changed or completed. The lines go to your model with the task, credential-redacted and bounded, are reused for at most a minute, and are not stored by Butler. The same helper binary also carries benchmark-only commands that create items titled with a benchmark marker in a local, non-syncing "OpenAssistBench" calendar and list and remove them afterwards; outside those two it removes only a marker-titled item the benchmark run itself created, never one that existed before it, has attendees or repeats. The app never calls them, and they run only when you run the automation benchmark yourself ([docs/BENCHMARK.md](docs/BENCHMARK.md)).
- A native event tap detects manual mouse/keyboard input to pause the agent and listens for Escape. It does not record the contents of manual keystrokes or mouse paths. Butler's own tagged input is excluded from takeover detection.
- The real desktop captures the selected display only during active runs, with one exception: with “Open apps as I say them” on (the default), saying “open …” takes one screenshot while you are still speaking, so the app you name can be opened before you finish. It stays in memory, is never sent to a model, and is written to the encrypted run record only as the first frame of the task it belongs to; otherwise it is discarded. It never leaves the Mac, in any privacy mode. Protected app windows are excluded; detected sensitive input or protected foreground surfaces suspend capture. Other visible content can still appear. Keep unrelated sensitive material off the selected display.
- Run content is encrypted locally with AES-256-GCM. The master key is wrapped by the operating system's secure storage. API keys are stored in encrypted configuration, never in run events. A compromised logged-in machine can still access an unlocked application.
- Pause, takeover and stop suspend new capture/input. Takeover does not install a user-keystroke recorder. Existing encrypted run frames remain until deleted.
- There is no telemetry sender and no default contribution. No CoArena endpoint is necessary for private use.
- Per-run contributions require review and an affirmative checkbox. You can choose statistics or sanitized task/action/correction data and synthetic tutorial images. Real native screenshots are excluded in this alpha. Sanitization is not anonymization and can miss contextual identifiers.
- Contribution requests carry a versioned consent record and checksum-bound manifest. A receipt is accepted only after every chunk and the complete bundle are verified. The service keeps data in quarantine and does not publish or train on it automatically.
- The reference service expires contributions after seven days during startup/hourly sweeps. Withdraw deletes the server contribution using a locally retained possession token. Local deletion is separate; neither operation securely erases SSD sectors or preexisting backups. Production backup/derived-data deletion is not implemented here.
- The browser preview keeps runs in memory only, loses them on reload, and cannot upload a contribution or store provider keys.

Before opening a contribution program, replace this draft with reviewed collection notices, retention/deletion policies, processor terms, rights handling and a clearly identified data controller/contact.
