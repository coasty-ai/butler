# Voice product contract

The subsequent Open Assist MVP specification narrows the user-facing product from the strategy PDF to the primary interaction: **hold a key → speak → release → watch the computer work**. Open Assist is the consumer app; CoArena is the intelligence/evaluation layer, and CoArena Gym packages reviewed workflows into environments and evaluations.

The app lives in the macOS menu bar. It shows a compact floating pill only while listening, working, paused, asking for approval, or briefly confirming completion. There is no dashboard, chat sidebar, model picker on the main surface, task builder, agent page, integration marketplace or prompt history on the main screen. Small settings and optional local review remain accessible through the tray. The browser page is an interactive preview, not the desktop's main screen.

## Interaction

1. Hold Option-Space. The native helper signals the controller's stop latch, emits the listening event and requests a haptic on supported hardware. The latch is lifted only for the one early step described under Data and measurement, and closed again right after it.
2. Start microphone capture and on-device streaming STT at key-down, so a quick “yes” is not clipped (a tap under 160 ms still opens text entry and discards the audio). Any reply the assistant is speaking stops first. Audio is never written to disk. Partial text is transient pill feedback.
3. Release. Capture continues for a 0.3 second tail, the pill shows “One moment…”, and final recognition is awaited for up to 1.8 seconds. Apple Speech segments that end while the key is held are kept and joined, not dropped. If the recognizer ends with an empty final marker, the latest nonempty hypothesis is kept as a recovered command. A hypothesis that never finalized is offered in the text pill as “Send this?” and never acts by itself. Recovered, merged and unconfirmed speech can never approve a pending consequential action.
4. Capture current screen context and begin the task. A known plan (a built-in intent or a learned skill) may run its steps first without model calls. Otherwise the model chooses one validated mouse/keyboard action, a launch-only `open_app` or an index-bound `open_file`, at a time.
5. Hold again to interrupt: the run pauses immediately. Final corrections revise the same run; stale in-flight responses cannot execute. “Wait” pauses; “Stop” cancels. These intents are read from the final transcript, not partials, so “Stop scrolling and click Save” stays a correction. Unrecognized speech keeps the run paused. Neither requires waiting for model inference.
6. Consequential actions and labelled controls outside a benign allow-list show an approval; unidentified targets are retried without input. Click Yes or hold and say “yes.” Spoken approval must match the pending action from listening start and pass the final confidence gate. “Not yet” pauses the task.
7. The pill shows a short summary of the result (“Done.” when none is safe to show), then hides.

## Spoken replies

Open Assist answers out loud when you talk to it (Settings → Voice replies: Off, When I talk to it, Always). It speaks only at moments that need you or close a task: approvals (“Send this message? Say yes or no.”), questions from the model, clarifications (“Open what?”), pauses it caused, the result, and failures. Push-to-talk turns get a brief acknowledgement (“On it.”); hands-free turns get a soft sound instead, because the microphone stays open for a follow-up. It never speaks while the microphone is capturing, never reads typed text, credentials, coordinates, full web addresses or file paths, and never says a phrase that would itself stop, pause, resume, approve or decline.

- **Mac voice (default):** Apple `AVSpeechSynthesizer` inside the native voice helper, on-device and free, using the best installed voice. Compact voices sound robotic; Settings offers a shortcut to download a free Premium voice.
- **Natural voice (free, on-device):** Kokoro-82M (Apache-2.0) through ONNX Runtime in an Electron utility process, after a one-time 332 MB download (pinned revisions, SHA-256 verified) from Hugging Face and GitHub. Nothing you say or type leaves the Mac. Pronunciation uses the misaki gold dictionary plus a small non-espeak fallback model, so no GPL code ships. The worker starts on first use or when you start talking, takes about 1.3 s to its first audio when cold and about 0.5 s per short sentence when warm, uses about 700 MB while loaded and exits after 5 minutes idle. Apple Silicon only; any failure before audio starts falls back to the Mac voice.
- **Natural voice (OpenAI, opt-in):** `gpt-4o-mini-tts` streamed as PCM from the main process to the helper. Allowed only with the Bring-your-own-model privacy mode and an OpenAI key. The reply text goes to OpenAI, it costs about $0.015 per spoken minute on your key, and the voice is AI-generated. Any failure before audio starts falls back to the Mac voice.
- **Interrupting:** holding Option-Space, the wake phrase, Escape, a click on the pill, Stop or Control-Option-Escape stops speech immediately inside the helper, before the app handles the event. In hands-free mode, listening pauses while the assistant speaks and for 0.8 seconds afterwards, so its own voice is not transcribed.

## Forgiving turn-taking

Hands-free turns end on several signals instead of a fixed pause. A turn ends when the words have stopped changing and the microphone has been quiet for long enough, and how long depends on whether the sentence sounds finished. With the default Normal patience: 2.0 s of stable words and 1.5 s of quiet for a finished-sounding command; 3.5 s and 2.5 s when it ends on a word like “and”, “to”, “can you”, “um” or a lone verb such as “Open”; 0.9 s and 0.7 s for “stop” or a short answer. Quick and Relaxed patience scale these (Settings → Listening). Quiet is measured against an adaptive noise floor, so soft trailing words still count as speech. Apple's recognizer segments are accumulated, so words before a long pause are not lost; stutters, fillers (“um”, “uh”) and repeated words are removed before the command reaches the model, and misheard wake echoes (“Hey sis”, “Hey assistant”) are stripped. Saying the wake phrase again part-way (“Assist, open Calendar and…”) starts the request over: only the words after the last wake phrase are the command. At the start of a new recognizer segment that is “Hey Assist”, or the bare name set apart by punctuation or followed by a task verb (“Assist open…”; not “Assist me…”, “assist the customer…”, and never the misheard echoes such as “his assistant” or “hi sis”, which are ordinary speech there). Inside one segment it is “hey assist”, or the bare name when the request's opening words repeat after it. A restarted turn counts as more than one segment, so it can never approve.

A lone fragment such as “Open” gets a question (“Open what?”) instead of starting a run. With follow-up listening on, the assistant keeps listening without the wake phrase for a short window after a turn (3 seconds, to catch “…and search”), after it asks a question (8 seconds) and after an approval prompt (8 seconds). Words added before the run has acted amend the task instead of counting as a correction. A spoken approval inside a follow-up window needs higher recognition confidence, a single recognizer segment and the same pending action, and it only covers low-risk prompts: opening an app or file, quitting an app, and navigation clicks such as “Next page” or “Learn more”. Anything that sends, submits, changes settings, deletes or installs needs a click, push-to-talk or the wake phrase.

A short tap opens text entry; Command-Shift-Space provides a fallback. Escape and Control-Option-Escape are native stop controls during real runs. Escape also cancels ongoing speech. Manual mouse/keyboard input pauses the run instantly (pointer jitter under 3 px is ignored). It continues on its own from a fresh observation once the user lets go: about a second after pointer movement or scrolling, three seconds after clicks or typing, unless anything else happened meanwhile. “Continue” still resumes at any time. When the agent cannot identify a control it asks the user to click it, and continues a second after that click. Dismissing an untouched text-entry pill opened by a tap during a working run resumes it; other dismissals leave a held run paused. With hands-free mode off, voice steering requires holding the shortcut. With optional “Hey Assist” enabled, the native helper listens locally for a phrase at the start of an utterance, plays a soft sound, opens the same pill, and ends the turn with the patience rules above. The command window times out after ten seconds without words (Normal) and ends at 45 seconds. A nonempty final recognition normally supplies the command. An empty final after the explicit silence/release endpoint may recover the latest nonempty hypothesis; it is marked separately and cannot approve an action. Outside a follow-up window, say the wake phrase again for corrections, stopping and approval. Ambient speech outside a window cannot act, and follow-up approvals carry the stricter checks above. The microphone remains active until disabled in the menu bar or the app quits. Existing settings migrate with this mode off.

## Data and measurement

Local encrypted records connect intent → action → finalized correction → revised action → outcome through one run ID and action index. Correction timestamps remain local. Per-run opt-in contribution can include sanitized task/action/correction text; raw audio is never included, and real desktop screenshots are excluded until image sanitization is implemented and validated. There is no default telemetry upload.

The proposed north-star metric is the number/share of active users initiating at least five real tasks from the shortcut per local day. Count a new task once; exclude tutorials, retries within a run, approval utterances and correction utterances. Track correction success, task completion and time to first useful action alongside it. Central collection and a users/day report are not implemented; they require a separate opt-in measurement design.

The <100 ms listening-feedback target is a target, not a measured result. Measure key-down→visible pill, key-down→audio-ready, release→final transcript, release→first action, and interrupt→last emitted input at p50/p95 on target Macs. Avoid speculative input before the user finishes speaking, with one exception (Settings → Listening → “Open apps as I say them”, on by default): when a request starts with “open”, “launch”, “switch to”, “pull up” or “bring up” and an app name, and the partial transcripts settle on that name (a following “and”, “then” or comma heard twice or held for 0.3 s, or the words stopping at the exact app name for 0.6 s), that one verified, non-protected app is brought forward before the sentence ends. Nothing is typed, clicked, sent or opened by address early; a change of mind (“or”, “no”, “actually”, “in Chrome”, “Slack's…”) cancels it for the turn, nothing is spoken about it, and an app brought forward is never switched away again. The fast-start line “Opening Slack.” is then replaced by the plain acknowledgement. The optional hands-free implementation uses rolling Apple on-device recognition, not a dedicated low-power wake-word model; wake latency, false activations and battery impact still require microphone testing.

No proactive assistant, schedules, mobile app or integration platform is part of this MVP. Bounded current/recent context is included: app/window title, selection/static accessibility text, up to 12 open/recently observed windows, eight observed document basenames and the last three local tasks. Local learned memory adds relevant past episodes, corrections, apps, folders and Spotlight-listed files, and replays predictable procedures ([MEMORY.md](MEMORY.md)). It is not a continuous screen recorder, and it never reads file contents. Spoken replies, including the free on-device natural voice, are described above.
